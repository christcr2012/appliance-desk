## Rentals (agreements)

## Visits, dispatch and the driver view

![Dispatch](owner-guide/screenshots/dispatch.png)

**What it's for.** **Visits** (`/desk/jobs`) schedules deliveries, installs, swaps, removals and maintenance, optionally tied to an agreement (customer and machines fill in). The **Driver view** shows only a staff member's own jobs, with address, machines, notes and photo upload.

**Change without code.** Settings → Visits and scheduling (usual length, whether staff may work unassigned jobs) and Pickups and deliveries.

**If it fails.** A completion that cannot be saved keeps every answer on screen. Anything not delivered or not picked up creates a follow-up task for each item so it is never lost.

## Inventory, parts, purchases and suppliers

![Inventory](owner-guide/screenshots/inventory.png)

**What it's for.** **Inventory** lists every physical machine with asset number (like WASH-0001, never reused), serial, condition and status. After a pickup a machine goes to **Awaiting inspection**; check each item, and if all pass it goes to **Being cleaned** until you press **Cleaning done** and it becomes Available; if anything fails it goes to repair with a draft repair visit. A saved inspection cannot be edited; add a correction note. The **Who has it** panel shows which customer holds it. **Purchases** records a receipt once with one row per machine (model, serial, price), photos of every page, and the seller's tax split to the cent; the **Audit pack** lists everything you bought in a date range. **Parts** are kept by model with counts; **Suppliers** and **Purchase orders** (Draft, Ordered, Received) add to those counts as shipments arrive. Every count change is written to a permanent history; use **Undo** or **Archive** rather than delete.

**Selling parts (new, off until you use it).** On **Parts**, press **Sell to customers** on a part, type the price you sell it for, and (if you usually buy it to resell) tick "Usually bought to resell". On a new purchase order, a part you sell gets a box "Bought to resell to customers": tick it when you gave the supplier your Colorado resale certificate so you did not pay sales tax (your CPA confirms the exact form). If you later use some of those parts yourself on a repair or install, the app records the use tax for you and lists it under Taxes → Use tax; if your tax address is not set up yet it waits, shows on Today, and is recorded automatically when setup is done. Selling to customers comes in the next update.

**Shop sales (new).** Under **Money → Shop sales** (`/desk/sales`), press **New sale**, choose what is being sold, who it is for (or "Walk-in"), and whether it is picked up or delivered. The sales tax is figured for you: a pickup uses the shop's tax areas, a delivery uses the customer's address. The sale saves as an invoice and the items come off the shelf at the same moment. Open the sale to **record a payment** (cash, check or bank transfer) or, if nothing has been paid yet, **cancel it** and the items go back on the shelf. Selling below the listed price needs a short reason. A sale is refused with plain reasons until you have told Taxes whether "Items sold" and "Used appliance sale" are taxable.

**Retiring an appliance — sell, strip for parts, scrap or throw away (new).** A retired appliance can never be rented again. On its page, **What's next for it?** lets you choose: sell it, strip it for parts, scrap it, throw it away, or donate/other (with a note). You can change your mind until you mark it done. **Strip it for parts** lets you add the parts you kept to your shelf (at $0 each, because their cost is already in what you paid for the appliance) and asks what happened to the rest. The **Retired** tab under Equipment (`/desk/inventory/retired`) lists every retired appliance, lets you mark a truckload done at once, and holds **Scrap money received**: enter each scrap-yard check once for the whole load, not per appliance. It goes in as "Scrap sales" income. Dump fees are an ordinary expense in the "Dump and disposal fees" category. Today shows one reminder when a retired appliance has waited more than 30 days (you can change the number, or enter 0 for never). Choosing a plan changes nothing in your books. Selling a retired appliance through Shop sales is not built yet.

**The tax question has no default.** Only the owner answers it; "I'll check the receipt later" creates a dated To do, and another state's tax creates a CPA question.

**Change without code.** Appliance types and pictures (Settings → Products and pricing), sets and packages, record-keeping years (starts at 7; a receipt a filed return relies on can never be removed).

**If it fails.** A receipt photo that cannot be stored is refused with a plain message and nothing partial is saved. Using more parts than the count shows is refused instead of dropping to zero.

## Books — profit, balance and appliance payback

From **Books → Profit, balance and payback**, choose the Colorado date period and accrual versus cash reporting. Accrual reads the true posted journal income/discounts/expense/depreciation by book date. Cash includes only posted payments attached to a real receipt and an original invoice account/tax allocation; unmatched customer money and deposits are not booked as income. If a source is ambiguous, it appears in the needs-review warning and is excluded from the reported total until reconciled.

The balance screen shows existing checking, card, Stripe/undeposited funds, customer balances, tax/deposit liabilities, equity and retained results. It keeps retired equipment pending disposition as an **asset**, with accrued depreciation netted once. The appliance payback screen divides actual invoiced rent across physical appliances assigned at that invoice period's start, then accounts for sourced repairs; missing costs remain unknown. Remaining payback months at the last six-month rent pace are a **projection**, never guaranteed cash. OWNER/ADMIN can view; STAFF/CUSTOMER cannot access these private reports. ## Fixed assets, management depreciation and CPA evidence

From **Books → Rental equipment and book depreciation**, OWNER can enroll an existing appliance with a documented positive acquisition cost and explicitly attest an in-service date on or after the Books start date. ADMIN can read; staff/customers cannot access Books. This records management book evidence without moving money or changing the federal tax basis.

The default management straight-line schedule is 60 months and 0% salvage, overridable for new assets; use an individual asset's future-only policy form to change the remaining useful life without rewriting posted journals. **Post depreciation** for completed Colorado months; the ledger uses unique journal events and advances zero-cent months in a durable checkpoint. A complete physical Fleet retirement, followed by the completed depreciation postings, allows OWNER to stage its actual retirement intent and preserve remaining carrying value in the Retired pending disposition **asset** account. This is not sale income, tax gain or a premature disposal loss. Final sale/disposal is a separately approved later feature.

The CPA evidence form records classification, source date/reference, original adjusted-basis inputs, signed adjustments, allowed/allowable depreciation and section 179/bonus separately; UNKNOWN remains a valid state. There is no automatic recapture or income-tax due estimate. CPA decision IN-56 and real-accounting-book reconciliation remain outstanding. Missing acquisition journal, conflicting values, stale form version, missing retirement evidence or completed-month checkpoint fails closed; reconcile the underlying source without editing immutable journals.

## Billing, deposits and statements

![Billing](owner-guide/screenshots/billing.png)

**What it's for.** Rent is charged through Stripe, agreement by agreement. **Billing → Statements** lists every customer with a balance; a statement groups invoices by property and has a CSV. **+ Record payment** records a check, cash or bank transfer (leave "Apply to" on all open invoices, oldest first, for one check covering several); extra becomes account credit. **Write off** marks an uncollectible invoice without deleting it. **Send payment link** shows you the exact message first (or **Copy link**); **Take payment by phone** opens Stripe's own page. **Deposits** lists what you hold and have not decided; **Decide** gives it back in full or in part with a written reason. **Money decisions** on an invoice lets you refund or pay with account credit, and every decision repeats the amount in dollars and words for you to tick. **Waiting for Stripe** shows requests Stripe has not finished; there is nothing to press, the system retries every night. **Held payments** needs your review. Late fees are added once a day using each agreement's own terms and are never charged to a card automatically, and never while a bank payment is being confirmed.

**Bank payments.** A customer who pays by bank transfer waits up to 4 banking days. The bill shows **Being confirmed** (Bills has its own tab for these); it is not counted as paid and not chased as late, and no late fee is added meanwhile. Nothing for you to do. If a bank payment is still waiting after your number of banking days (**Settings › Bank payments**, starting value 6, with **Restore recommended**), a calm item appears on Today. If Stripe says it cleared but it isn't on the bill, press **Record it now** (Today, or Billing › Reconciliation › Bank payments Stripe says cleared); it records once even if pressed twice.

**Late fees.** One rule for every rental: a fee only when a bill is more than 10 days late (the fee day is day 11), at most $15, once per late bill. The customer is warned first: a few days before the fee day (**Settings › Late fee warning**, starting value 2) they get a message with the amount and the date. No delivered warning, no fee. If the warning could not go out, Today shows "No late fee for … — they weren't warned yet" and the bill page offers **Send the warning**, **I told them by phone**, or **Skip the fee** (with a reason). A fee is never added to the bill the customer is paying and never while a payment is in progress (a bank payment being confirmed, or a phone payment just started); if that payment fails, the customer is warned again and the fee day moves. The fee is its own charge, put on the customer's **next** bill (or on a separate one-line bill if the rental ended), and the bill page says "Late fee will be added to the next bill on …". Waive it any time with a reason (before it is billed it is never billed; after, the customer gets a credit). A customer marked **Active-duty military** (customer page › Account) never gets an automatic fee. Starting values show as "not confirmed" until you confirm them.

**Change without code.** Settings → Rental policies and Products and pricing (fees, tax rate, prices, prepay discounts, the 12-month free-month bonus, payment-link wording). Only agreements signed after the change are affected.

**If it fails.** A failed automatic charge puts the invoice in Delinquent and a **Payment failed** item on Today with the unpaid dollars; the customer can use **Pay now** in their account, a replay never charges or records twice, and the item clears when the payment is applied. For unclear Stripe states read `docs/runbooks/UNKNOWN-STRIPE-STATE.md`; for a provider outage `docs/runbooks/PROVIDER-OUTAGE.md`.

## Taxes

**What it's for.** Money → Taxes has four tabs: **To file**, **Use tax**, **Rates & areas** and **Setup** (including customers who do not pay tax, what gets taxed, the audit pack and record keeping). Each return starts with one line, "File and pay $X on Colorado's sales tax website (SUTS) by <date>" with an Open SUTS button, then the numbers to copy in order (use tax follows the DR 0252 form). Press **I filed and paid it** with the date and confirmation number once you have. Appliance Desk never files or pays for you. Purchase tax on appliances needs your business address confirmed under Setup and your tax-election answers first. Every return also shows "File and pay on <date>", the day the timing rule suggests acting (90% through the time allowed, at least 3 business days before the deadline; you can change both under Taxes › Rules). **Tax and fee rules** (`/desk/sales-tax/rules`) lists every tax and fee number with what it is now, what it changes to next, where it came from and when it was checked, and its history. Researched numbers are marked "not confirmed" until you or your CPA confirms them. **Delivery area** (`/desk/sales-tax/area`) lists every place you deliver to and whether it is ready: its rent-tax rule decided and, where the city requires one, its license on file in Business records. An address in a place that is not ready cannot be quoted, ordered or put on the schedule — the app says why in plain words — and a "Get ready to deliver in …" item appears on Today only when a real customer address needs that place. Use **Check an address** on that screen to test any customer address before promising a delivery. The only shortcut is yours: when the sole problem is "rules not decided", you may override one order with a reason (it is written to Activity); a missing city license can never be overridden. **Compliance** (`/desk/compliance`, linked from the Taxes home page as "Beyond sales tax") is everything that is not sales tax: federal and Colorado estimated tax and the yearly return, 1099s, the Weld County property declaration, the Secretary of State periodic report, city licenses and the payroll items that wake up when you hire. It has four sections — **Coming up**, **Needs you**, **Done** and **Not needed** (the last shows the items checked and ruled out, with the reason and source). Each filing moves prepare → approve → filed under your approval only: approving binds the exact numbers prepared, so changed numbers must be reviewed again, and nothing is ever filed twice or without you. **Business facts** (`/desk/compliance/facts`) holds your entity type, report month, truck weight and employee count; your EIN is stored encrypted, only its last 4 digits are ever shown, and the EIN box stays off until the encryption key is set (see your owner checklist). **1099s and W-9s (new).** On a supplier's page you can ask for a W-9: the supplier gets a one-time link (works once, 14 days) to type their tax ID or upload a signed W-9; the number is stored encrypted and only its last 4 digits ever show. Paying a 1099 supplier with no W-9 on file puts a To do on Today with the 24% backup-withholding warning. When a year's 1099 filing comes up under Compliance, preparing it adds up the year's payments from the books on the same basis as the year-end review, leaves any category you haven't classified out of the boxes (you classify it on the filing page — the app never guesses), and after you approve, the filing page offers the IRS portal file (CSV for the IRIS Taxpayer Portal, under your TCC from the owner checklist) and a filled Copy B PDF per recipient. Your business name and mailing address for the payer block live on Business facts. If a customer pays more than $10,000 in cash — all at once or across related payments within 12 months — a Form 8300 filing appears on its own, due 15 days after the payment that crossed the line, with a filled draft to check and file on the BSA E-Filing system. **Income tax (new).** The yearly income-tax filing under Compliance prepares your **accountant packet** from the posted books: every account is mapped to its Schedule C line (anything unmapped is listed by name under Other expenses — nothing is dropped), the card charges are tied out against the 1099-K Stripe sent you, and the depreciation (Form 4562) and appliance-sale (Form 4797) worksheets ride along, each marked as an estimate for your CPA to confirm. Approve the numbers on the filing page, then download the packet as a PDF or CSV and send it to your accountant. **Quarterly estimates** (federal and Colorado) are worked out by the safe-harbor method from last year's tax — type last year's federal tax and income and Colorado tax once under Business facts ("One year's tax figures"), and each quarter's filing shows the amount, a link to pay at IRS Direct Pay or Colorado Revenue Online, and a filled paper voucher you can download (type your name and Social Security number when you download it; they are used only to fill that voucher and are never saved). A first year with no prior-year figures shows no amount — the app never invents one; use an estimate from your CPA. The app never pays or files income tax for you. **Colorado and local filings (new).** The Weld County property declaration is built from your appliance records as they stood on January 1: every unit you owned is listed with its cost, the customer renting it and the rent (a set's rent is never split between its two machines — the set's own price is shown). If the total original cost is at or under the county's $56,000 exemption, the filing page says a declaration looks unnecessary — because that total counts only the appliances the app tracks, it closes as "No declaration needed" only when you confirm the tracked fleet is everything the business owns, with the numbers kept for your records. Over the line, approving it unlocks the full spreadsheet to file with the Assessor by April 15, plus the filled county forms (DS 056 and DS 060) once the county publishes that year's printing — it re-issues them every year with the year printed on them, and only the same year's printing is safe to file. The forms never carry your tax ID or a signature, so sign them yourself. Tools and office equipment are not tracked in the app yet, so add those on the form by hand. Business facts now also holds your trade name (an LLC never renews one — the app asks for the name once and schedules nothing), your Greeley license date (the city license is free and renews every two years on that date), your truck's registration month and USDOT number (the federal update month and year are read off the number itself), and your registered agent's name and address, which the Secretary of State report page shows as exact values to type. **Hiring (new).** Payroll stays asleep until you record employees on Business facts. Add your first employee's start date there too: the Compliance page then shows a **hiring checklist** (EIN, W-4 and I-9, the Colorado new-hire report due 20 days after the start date, the state withholding and unemployment accounts, FAMLI registration, workers' comp, posters and paid sick leave) whose statuses come from your facts and filings, never from tick boxes, and the payroll filings (941, 940, W-2s, Colorado withholding, UI and FAMLI) wake up in the usual sections. Payroll itself runs through a payroll service — that choice is an open decision (DQ-CMP-PAYROLL-HOW, starting value: service). Under the checklist, **Record payroll in the books** imports the provider's summary CSV: one row per pay run (pay_date, gross_wages, employer_taxes, amount_withdrawn) posts wages and the business's payroll taxes against the bank withdrawal, so the books match the payroll filings. The same file can never be imported twice, a row that does not add up posts nothing, and the file must hold summary totals only — never employee names or Social Security numbers. **Write-offs and tax benefits (new).** **Tax benefits** (`/desk/sales-tax/benefits`, under Taxes › Setup) shows, for the year you pick, every write-off and benefit the app can find in your own numbers: depreciation and expensing totals, the truck (mileage rate vs actual costs, both shown), the home office (simplified vs the regular method, both shown), self-employment tax and its deductible half, health-insurance premiums, retirement (SEP-IRA vs solo 401(k) estimates with their deadlines), the 20% qualified business income estimate, start-up costs, the Colorado enterprise zone and county property lines, and your ordinary deductions account by account — each with what it is, the amount, the condition the app checked, the deadline, and "your CPA confirms this estimate", because every number is an estimate from the rule book, never tax advice. Purchases are classified when you record them: an invoice line at or under the de minimis limit is expensed when that year's election is on, otherwise the unit is capitalized (bonus depreciation by default, Section 179 if you switch the preference, and never bonus for a purchase from a relative or your own other business — the purchase form asks), and the decision with its reason is written on the unit and never changes afterward. The page also holds your **capitalization policy**: the app writes it from the rule book, you sign it on screen, it is kept in Business records, and a December To do asks you to re-sign for the next year. **Write-off settings** on the same page hold the answers only you can give: the business-use percent for shared accounts like phone and internet (until you answer, 0% of those accounts is deducted), whether the business sits in a Colorado enterprise zone, the bonus-vs-Section-179 preference, and the de minimis election per year. If buying late in the year would push too much of the year's equipment into the last three months, an October/November To do warns you before you buy more. **Elections and year-end planning (new).** The same page's **Elections** section shows the statements that ride on the year's accountant packet: the de minimis safe harbor statement (it appears once that year's capitalization policy is signed; it names the business, its address and only the EIN's last 4 digits — never the full number), the start-up costs note, and — when you check a class — the statement electing out of bonus depreciation for that class of property. You or your accountant can set that elect-out choice on the page; every change your accountant makes is listed for you in Books, under Changes by your accountant. The packet prints the return's due date and the extension date alongside the statements. Every November 15 a **Year-end tax planning** To do appears with the year's facts so far — tentative profit, retirement room, the mid-quarter picture and what has been expensed under the safe harbor — plus the questions to take to your CPA. It is information for planning, never advice to buy anything.

**Change without code.** Filing accounts, rates and areas, tax policy (owner only); administrators can review only. Admins can invite staff only if you turn on **Admins may invite staff**. **Make admin** / **Make staff** signs that person out and emails them. **Remove access** signs them out everywhere and **Restore access** brings them back. You cannot remove or change yourself.

**Giving your accountant sign-in (ACCT-1).** In the same **Invite a team member** form, pick the access level **Accountant (CPA or tax professional)** — only you (the owner) can invite an accountant. Add their firm if you like, and an **Access ends on** date (the starting value is the next May 1, after tax season; or tick **Keep until I remove it** and you will get a reminder to re-check the access about every six months). An accountant who signs in sees **only** `/desk/books`, `/desk/sales-tax`, `/desk/compliance`, `/desk/reports`, the CPA questions in `/desk/settings/decisions`, and the business records you share — never customers, bills, the logins vault, or anything else. They always need a second sign-in step and their device is never remembered. They can prepare filings and mark them **Reviewed**, post adjusting entries in a month, set which Schedule C line an account feeds, confirm or void bank matches, and add files to records you shared — but only you can approve or file a return, and only you can reverse their adjusting entries. On `/desk/books` you get a **Changes by your accountant** list in plain words, and **Today** reminds you when there are changes you have not looked at or an end date is coming up. To share one business record, open it in `/desk/paperwork` and use **Share with your accountant**; shared records still hide their full numbers. **Change end date**, **Replace** (remove them and start a fresh invite), **Remove access** and **Restore access** (restoring always needs a new end date) are all on their row in **Settings → Team**. After the end date passes, sign-in stops on its own.

**Sign-in codes (new).** When someone sets up two-step sign-in (**Desk → Settings → Security → Set up**) they choose an authenticator app, a **text message code** or an **email code**, whichever you allow for their level. Under **Settings → Security → How each access level may get its second step** you tick what each level may use; the authenticator app is always allowed, and the starting value gives you and admins authenticator or text, and staff all three. Email codes are weaker for you and admins (the forgot-password link goes to the same email), which is why they start off for you. **Text codes are off** until you tick **Send sign-in codes by text** (separate from customer texting; it only works on the live site once the phone-provider keys are in place). On **My account → Second step** a person checks their phone with a text code and can switch between text and email by proving a code from the new method. At sign-in, a **Text or email code** tab has "Send me a code"; codes last 5 minutes. If a text cannot be sent, use the authenticator app or a backup code. Changing your rules never signs anyone out: people already on a method keep it and are asked to switch.

**Recovery and notices (new).** If a staff member or admin loses their phone or forgets how they sign in, open **Settings → Team**, find them and choose **Reset sign-in**. They are signed out everywhere, their second step is turned off, and they get an email with a link (works once) to choose a new password; then they set up the second step again. You cannot reset your own sign-in this way or another owner's; keep your backup codes somewhere separate from your phone (the owner steps are in the account recovery runbook). Under **Settings → Security → Wrong passwords** you set how many wrong passwords one account may have (starting value 10 within 15 minutes) before it waits that long; the person is emailed, and Forgot password still works. People on your team now also get a short email, with no sign-in link, after a forgotten-password reset, when sign-in is paused, when they sign in from a new device, and when their second step changes.

**Remember this device (new).** Under **Settings → Security → Remember devices** you set how many days a ticked "Remember this device" skips the code on that phone or computer (starting value 30 days; 0 means always ask). At sign-in, the box is offered after the code and is off unless ticked; it is never offered on the first two-step setup. Lowering the number shortens every remembered device at once. A changed password, a changed sign-in email, a changed second step, turning two-step off, a reset, a removed or changed access level, or signing out everywhere always asks for the code again. The first sign-in from a device is only recorded; the second phone of the same kind counts as new and gets the email. Customers get that email only while **Send emails to customers** is on; while it is off, the missed email is recorded. The question is also in **Decisions & reviews** for you to confirm the 30 days.

**Job roles (new).** Under **Settings → Team → Job roles** you decide which screens a kind of job can open. Four come ready: Driver / technician and Office (staff), Bookkeeper (admin, cannot change business settings) and Manager (admin, everything). Pick a job role for someone in the **Job role** column or when you invite them; they are signed out so it shows at once. A job role can only take screens away: staff can never be given money or settings screens, and people with no job role see everything their level allows, exactly as before. Edit a role to tick or untick screens, **Archive** one nobody uses, or **Restore recommended** to put a starting role back.
