## Federal consumer leasing analysis — Regulation M, 12 CFR Part 1013

**Confirmed from CFPB's current Jan. 1, 2026 rule and official interpretations:** This is **not a rule limited to cars or housing**. It governs qualifying consumer leases of **personal property**, such as standalone washers and dryers, regardless of whether the lessee can buy the goods (12 CFR 1013.2(e), (l)). Cars/furniture are examples, not an exhaustive list.

A transaction may be covered where:
1. A **natural person** rents primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, not agriculture or business/commercial purposes or under a contract to an organization.
2. The **initial binding term exceeds four months** (thus ordinary **six- and twelve-month** household contracts should be screened). A genuine month-to-month arrangement is ordinarily outside Regulation M even if it continues a year; however, a penalty for ending before more than four months can bring it within coverage. The operative obligations, not the heading, determine the term.
3. The **total contractual obligation is at most $73,400 during 2026**, including required nonrefundable amounts as defined in the commentary; check adjusted thresholds in future years.
4. The company meets the covered **lessor** definition: regularly leasing/offering/arranging covered personal-property leases, including the **more-than-five times in the preceding or current calendar year** criterion. Do not treat a low initial count as permission to avoid designing compliant forms for a growing business.

**Not to confuse with:** Rental of **real estate** itself is not a personal-property consumer lease under this part. The rule specifically excludes certain personal property *incidental to the real-property lease* if the lessee has no end-of-term liability beyond abnormal wear and tear and no purchase option; standalone appliance leases with their own vendor/agreement generally require separate analysis. The CFPB also identifies specific equipment-incidental-to-service exclusions; including repair and maintenance in an appliance rental does not itself establish that exception.
