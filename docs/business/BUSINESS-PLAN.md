# Robinson Appliance Rentals business plan

Planning edition • October 7 2026 • Owner review draft

## Purpose and direction

Robinson Appliance Rentals will provide dependable laundry appliances, installation and ongoing service to households and property managers in the Greeley and Evans area. The recommended launch is a small, serviceable refurbished fleet with clear monthly pricing, scheduled delivery and disciplined cost tracking. Premium new equipment and recurring referral partnerships follow only after their economics and service capacity are proven.

This is an operating plan, not a claim of current revenue, established demand or secured funding. The consultant's proposed “Northern Colorado Appliance Rentals” name does not replace Robinson Appliance Rentals. Verify the exact registered entity and insurance coverage before publishing legal documents. The repository describes a prelaunch business; there is no verified customer, inventory or financial dataset supplied with the briefing.

## Customers and value

| Segment | Need | Offer and acquisition approach |
|---|---|---|
| Local households with suitable hookups | Laundry access without buying and maintaining a set | Refurbished rentals; transparent quote; local search, referrals and community outreach |
| Landlords and property managers | Less appliance administration across their properties | Property-paid portfolio account with unit-level schedules and consolidated statements |
| Residents referred by property managers | A convenient provider at move-in | Resident-paid rental with a documented referral relationship |
| Students and temporary workers | A rental that matches a short stay | Limited local flexible offer priced for delivery, removal and vacancy costs |
| Customers who value unused equipment | Verified first-use machines | Premium offer only when qualifying stock and replacement terms are confirmed |

Market size, competitor pricing, purchase costs, partner interest and willingness to pay remain unvalidated. Before scaling, collect actual quotes from suppliers and insurers, interview local property managers and record the conversion and service cost of a small pilot. Do not publish unsupported claims about competitors, faster property leasing or market leadership.

## Products and commercial policy

The existing base-price defaults are $35 per individual appliance and $60 per set per month. Current term discounts mean these figures are not necessarily the final quoted price. The consultant's six-month $60 offer must be introduced as an explicit net-price offer if selected, not layered on top of an automatic discount by accident.

The candidate catalog in [Offers and pricing](OFFERS-AND-PRICING.md) adds refurbished, verified-new and local flexible offers. Fees, deposits, terms, discounts and eligibility remain owner-configurable. A deposit is refundable security, not revenue or available profit. Rent-to-own stays a separately reviewed future offer; this plan does not silently convert ordinary rentals into ownership contracts.

Keep delivery, installation and removal as separate quote lines because the existing system already supports them. A proposed $50 connection charge can be an installation fee; relabeling it does not decide its tax treatment. Taxes are determined by the service address and approved jurisdiction rules.

## Operations and capacity

Acquire equipment against verified demand and a defined reserve for repairs and replacement. Prefer maintainability, available parts, inspection results and total lifecycle cost over brand or timer style alone. Record condition, acquisition cost, purchase tax evidence, serial number and first-use evidence. An open-box or returned unit is not automatically eligible for a “never used” promise.

Use scheduled service windows and route grouping. Do not promise same-day installation or a 24–48-hour repair guarantee before capacity supports it. Chris's available work hours, lifting assistance, vehicle capacity, storage and technician support set the initial weekly limit; software should expose that capacity rather than assume an unrestricted calendar.

Every installation requires an approved checklist, compatible connections, new specified safety components, permission/access evidence where needed and a recorded functional test. Unsafe conditions stop the installation and create a follow-up job. Delivery completion, actual custody and billing remain tied to existing domain workflows.

## Sales and partnerships

Start with a truthful website and local outreach using a single quote process. Capture the source of each inquiry, issued quote, signed agreement and first collected rental payment. Use bounded promotions with an end date and usage cap, not permanent hidden discounts.

Offer property-paid accounts first through the existing customer/property/statement model. Introduce commercial master documents without replacing per-address rental agreements or duplicating billing. Test resident-paid referral commissions only after a written partner agreement, disclosure review and a viable margin calculation. Commission is earned from eligible collected rent, not merely an active account.

## Financial strategy

Use the [financial model](FINANCIAL-MODEL.md) as a transparent scenario, replacing assumptions with actual receipts and costs before any financing or expansion decision. Acquisition price alone is not the deployment investment. Include refurbishment, purchase tax, installation supplies, transport, labor, storage, insurance, software, payment fees, bad debt and a return trip.

Growth should be funded only after reserving cash for deposits owed back, tax liabilities, committed work and maintenance. Do not use customer deposits to make the operating model appear profitable. Neither paying off an appliance nor receiving a setup fee eliminates continuing costs.

No funding amount is asserted. Calculate funding as initial fleet and setup costs plus cash runway and contingency, less committed owner funding. Borrowing terms, depreciation and tax elections require their own review. No subscription or infrastructure purchase is authorized by this plan.

## Milestones and decision gates

| Stage | Evidence needed to progress |
|---|---|
| Prepare | Verified business identity, insurance, reviewed rental/access terms, approved tax configuration, defined capacity and a truthful price sheet |
| Pilot | Suggested 5–10 refurbished sets, only if affordable and supportable; record every cost, service visit, inquiry source and collected payment |
| Review after 90 operating days | Contribution after delivery/removal, utilization, arrears, repair time, complaints and available owner hours; 90 days is a review date, not a promised payback |
| Expand local fleet | Positive contribution at actual costs, enough cash after liabilities, service response within the published promise |
| Add premium or commercial pilot | Verified supply, economically supportable written offer, partner authority and clear responsibility for bills and access |
| Expand service territory | Route cost and response capacity proven for the new area; jurisdiction tax coverage configured |

## Management rhythm and risks

Each working day, review Today for undelivered agreements, blocked billing, access issues, maintenance and failed provider operations. Weekly, review route capacity, stock, overdue accounts and quotes. Monthly, reconcile deposits, receipts, expenses, tax obligations and partner commissions before approving growth spending.

The largest practical risks are underpriced service, unreliable acquisition quality, tax assumptions, unsafe installation, denied access, concentration in a few property accounts and time pressure on the owner. Address them with evidence, conservative promises, reserves, account limits and exception workflows. Track the plan's assumptions as decisions rather than quietly turning them into customer commitments.
