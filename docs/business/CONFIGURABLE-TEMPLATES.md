# Configurable business and agreement templates

Owner direction recorded October 7 2026

## Intended experience

The business plan describes starting choices. The application should let the owner turn those choices into reusable templates, change them from the owner account and use different options for different customers. The examples are suggestions, not permanent code values.

For example, duplicate “Standard refurbished rental,” rename it “Local nine-month offer,” choose a nine-month term, set its price and deposit, select a supported renewal policy and edit the service wording. Preview both the customer document and the resulting billing/ending rules before publishing. A particular customer may receive a permitted custom exception, with an approval and reason.

## What can be configured

| Template or preset | Owner controls |
|---|---|
| Residential rental | Supported term choices, monthly versus fixed duration, renewal and early-ending options, notice timing within legal limits, deposit and service/replacement wording |
| Commercial master | Portfolio/volume terms, payer responsibilities, service scope, schedule structure and approved clauses |
| Property authorization | Installation scope, signer capacity and lawful access wording; required legal/safety protections retained |
| Partner agreement | Fixed or percentage commission, qualifying rent, payout cadence/minimum, refund/dispute offsets and approved disclosures |
| Offer and promotion | Prices, separate fees, eligible stock/zones, duration options, discounts, usage caps and published dates |
| Installation policy | Applicable checks, required evidence and safe operational options within the reviewed safety standard |

Business terms use structured settings as well as editable text. A clause about ending fees must reflect the actual selected fee rule. A written promise cannot silently change the software calculation. The editor shows the relevant rule beside its wording and requires incompatible choices to be corrected before publication.

## Versions and customer agreements

An editable template is a reusable starting point. An issued agreement contains the exact selected template version, resolved settings, approved customer exceptions and customer-specific values. Signing freezes that copy and its evidence.

Changing a template affects later offers. It does not rewrite a current customer's accepted agreement, erase their deposit or change their renewal notice clock. If an unsigned quote needs refreshed terms, issue a replacement document and require the customer to review it again.

Templates can inherit named defaults from existing business settings or override them explicitly. The screen explains which source supplies each value. Inherited defaults are copied at issuance so future settings changes cannot alter the issued document.

## Owner workflow

Choose an existing template or create a draft from a suggested example. Duplicate it when a different customer group needs different terms. Edit supported rules, clause wording and optional sections; preview the resulting document and policy summary; obtain any required professional review; publish a version for selection in quotes. Retire an old version when it should no longer be offered.

“Restore suggested template” creates a fresh draft instead of destroying your edited version. Suggested examples are marked for review and are not enabled automatically. Approval of publication of repository planning documents does not activate customer contracts, prices, payments or communications.

Implementation is specified in Batch BP decision D-BP11. Existing settings, terms snapshots and approval machinery are reused; no separate conflicting source of business rules is introduced.
