# Appliance rental operating procedures

Planning edition • October 7 2026 • Proposed operating standard

## Before accepting a rental

Confirm the full service address, household or commercial payer, occupancy/ownership, equipment needs, term, delivery window and hookup compatibility. Ask about stairs, elevator, parking, door clearance, water shutoffs and dryer power/fuel. Do not collect unnecessary tenant or landlord information.

For a tenant, record their installation authorization and, where the property or approved policy requires it, a property-owner/manager authorization specific to that address and equipment. A tenant checkbox alone is not a landlord signature or proof of unrestricted entry. If authorization is denied, pause delivery and resolve the agreement and any payment through the existing cancellation/refund workflow.

Send a complete quote showing rent, deposit, fees, tax treatment and term. Do not reserve a delivery slot beyond real capacity or mark equipment rented before completed delivery. Unknown permission, unsafe access or unknown tax readiness creates a named task for the owner.

## Equipment intake and preparation

Record supplier/source, purchase date, serial/model, acquisition price, condition, purchase tax evidence and receipts in private storage. Missing cost or tax evidence is unknown, never zero. Follow Batch T for acquisition tax and Batch M for eventual sale, salvage or disposal; do not create parallel records.

Inspect and test using the manufacturer's instructions. Record defects, repair parts, labor and results. Clean the unit and confirm parts support. Maintain separate evidence for “purchased new,” “never deployed,” and “verified unused”; a returned, demonstrated or uncertain open-box unit cannot qualify just from a purchase label. A repair or unsafe finding prevents ready status.

Reserve a compatible appliance and required supplies before loading. New washer inlet hoses and suitable new dryer connection components are a proposed business standard; exact components and fitting must follow the specific machine's installation instructions and applicable professional requirements. Do not force an electrical cord rule onto gas dryers or modify unsafe premises to complete a delivery.

## Installation and handover

The job evidence must identify the actual installed asset, installer, address, time, policy version, component usage and outcome. Each required item is passed, failed or not applicable with a reason; a single “all done” tick is insufficient.

Record permission/access verification, condition before work, connection compatibility, new component usage, leveling, water/drain checks where applicable, venting and functional testing according to the manufacturer's procedure. Record a customer handover and safe reporting instructions. Photos support evidence but cannot replace a required check; protect home/interior photos as private customer data.

If the property is unsafe or a required step fails, stop work, record what was delivered versus installed, and create a follow-up. Do not mark an unsafe installation complete to trigger billing. Use the existing partial-delivery/custody model and owner resolution path; do not invent a new billing start event. A checklist gate must distinguish delivered-not-installed equipment so inventory remains truthful.

Safety failures have no “ignore and complete” owner override. A qualified correction and a new passing assessment resolve them. Non-safety administrative exceptions may be approved with evidence and a reason according to owner policy.

## Maintenance and incidents

Customer reports enter the existing maintenance queue and link to the asset, address and job. Triage suspected leaks, burning smells or electrical faults promptly and advise discontinuing use where safe; emergency situations go to the appropriate emergency service. Never promise a guaranteed response window that staffing cannot meet.

Schedule a visit or swap, track parts and labor, and preserve custody evidence for both machines. An incoming swap gets its own installation assessment. Resolve the request only with a recorded outcome and customer communication status. Failed or uncertain sends remain visible; do not mark a message delivered from a button click.

For alleged customer damage, record evidence and an owner decision; do not automatically take the deposit based on a technician label. Preserve the original charge, decision and refund/credit history.

## Renewal and return

Use the existing renewal workflow and continuity metadata. Keep the customer's cancel/return controls available. A voluntary retention contact 45 days before ending is separate from legally required renewal notices; it cannot replace them or restart their clocks.

Arrange pickup with lawful, current access arrangements. A landlord signature is not authorization to force entry or bypass an occupant dispute. If access is denied or contested, stop and escalate to the owner and counsel. Preserve attempted-contact and scheduling evidence without describing the task as an eviction or authorizing staff to change locks.

Complete pickup through the existing billing-end and deposit workflow. Inspect the returned asset; route it to available, maintenance or retirement based on evidence. Once deployed, a first-use promise is consumed permanently even if the customer cancels quickly.

## Routine controls

Daily: review failed deliveries, billing blockers, access disputes, urgent maintenance and provider uncertainty. Weekly: inspect capacity, supplies, repair backlog and overdue accounts. Monthly: reconcile cash, tax, deposits, partner obligations and asset costs; verify renewal notices and insurance/permit review dates. The owner sets thresholds and cadence in the app as those controls are implemented.
