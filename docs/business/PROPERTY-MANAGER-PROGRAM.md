# Property manager partnership program

Planning edition • October 7 2026 • Commercial proposal and policy brief

## Two separate arrangements

| Arrangement | Customer and payer | Property manager benefit | System treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Property-paid portfolio | Property owner or authorized management entity | Predictable equipment/service cost across units | Existing customer with multiple service addresses; commercial master document and per-address rental schedules |
| Resident-paid referral | Each resident | Documented referral compensation if approved | Resident retains private account; separate partner contract and commission ledger |

A partner can participate in both, but each rental has one identified payer and one explicit compensation policy. A referral relationship does not give the partner access to the resident's bills, contact details or account. A commercial payer may see its own account's locations and invoices through existing authorization rules.

## Property-paid offer

Candidate set prices are $60 for 1–4 sets, $52 for 5–9 and $45 for 10 or more. Candidate setup fees are $50, $25 and $0 per set respectively. These are negotiation examples, not approved prices or evidence of profitable volume. Clarify that residential per-job connection pricing differs from this per-set commercial example.

Qualifying volume means committed, billable sets in the same priced portfolio as defined in the signed master terms. Do not discount merely because a lead requested ten sets. Freeze a quoted volume band for its agreed term; vacancies or reductions affect the next reviewed quote unless the signed schedule already specifies a prospective repricing rule. Never retroactively charge back a discount.

The master document identifies the legal payer, authority of the signer, covered properties, volume commitment, services, payment terms, maintenance responsibilities, access coordination, insurance allocation, deposits and termination. Every equipment schedule identifies address/unit, assets, start/end rules, prices and the master version incorporated. A general master agreement does not itself allocate inventory or start a subscription.

Use existing consolidated statements and manual-payment allocation. One combined bank debit for several agreements is a separate future payment feature; do not claim the statement already provides it. Taxes remain attributable to each service location, even with one payer or a single statement.

## Resident-paid referral offer

Propose starting with a modest fixed commission, such as $5 per eligible set-month; the consultant's $5–$10 range is not an automatic entitlement. Only publish a rate after checking margin and reviewing the partner's authority, disclosure and contract obligations. Use “referral commission,” not “kickback,” in the agreement and accounts.

Identify a specific partner legal entity, agreement version, code, qualifying period, eligible rent definition, payout threshold and termination terms. Collect tax/payment information in private records, not this folder. Any disclosure required to residents is approved with counsel before activation.

Earn commissions only against allocated, settled rental receipts for the eligible service period, excluding tax, deposit, setup/delivery fees, penalties and refunded or disputed amounts. For a flat monthly commission, earned amount is the configured monthly commission multiplied by eligible net rent collected divided by the contractual eligible month's rent, capped at the monthly commission. Percentage commissions use eligible collected rent directly. Advance payments accrue commission into their respective service periods, not as twelve immediate monthly rewards.

Refunds or disputes create compensating negative ledger entries. If the partner was already paid, show the recoverable balance and offset it only as permitted by the signed partner terms. Do not rewrite paid history or debit the partner's bank account automatically.

## Payment control

Start with owner-approved manual payments and recorded references, not Stripe Connect. Reconcile payment evidence before marking a payable paid. This avoids introducing a second money-movement provider before there is a validated need.

A future payout integration requires its own design, recipient verification, approved fees, idempotency, reconciliation and explicit live-money authorization. “Processing” is not “Paid”; an uncertain result requires investigation, not retrying a fresh transfer.
